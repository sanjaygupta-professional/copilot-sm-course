You've just done something genuinely valuable: you've practised the hardest conversations in a space where the cost of getting it wrong is zero. That practice compounds.

The model to carry with you: latent conflict is cheap to address. Perceived conflict costs more. Felt conflict is expensive. Manifest conflict is the most costly of all. Your job is to move early — to name what you sense before it reaches the stage where it's obvious to everyone and morale is already damaged.

Here's a reflection: when the simulation got uncomfortable — when your practice partner deflected or escalated rather than opening up — what was your instinct? To push through, to back off, or to try a different approach? That instinct tells you something about your default conflict style. Knowing your default is the first step to choosing deliberately.

Your action: name the conflict or tension in your current team that you've been sensing but not addressing. Not diagnosing it publicly — just naming it to yourself, clearly, as a hypothesis. Then use the conflict recognition prompt from this module to look for evidence in your last three sprints of ceremony data. Does the data support your hypothesis? And if it does — what's one small, low-risk move you can make this sprint to acknowledge the tension and begin addressing it? Start there.
