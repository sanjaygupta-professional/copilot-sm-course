Here's a question that might reframe how you think about your tools: how much time do you spend copy-pasting? You synthesise notes in Co-pilot Chat, then open Word to write the summary. You analyse velocity in Chat, then switch to Excel to create the chart. You plan a presentation in Chat, then build it from scratch in PowerPoint.

What if those transitions just... disappeared?

This module is about Co-pilot inside your Microsoft 365 applications — not Co-pilot Chat on the web, but the AI assistant that lives inside the documents you're already creating. Same AI. Different context. Far more powerful for certain tasks.

You'll use Word Co-pilot to generate a professional Sprint 47 summary document — ready to share with stakeholders — in the time it takes to describe what you want. You'll use Excel Co-pilot to analyse velocity trends, calculate statistics, and create a chart comparing committed versus completed, without building a single formula by hand. And you'll use PowerPoint Co-pilot to create a five-slide sprint review deck from bullet-point data — giving you a polished, reviewable draft in minutes instead of hours.

The key skill you're building here is knowing which surface to use for which task. Chat is your thinking partner. In-app Co-pilot is your production assistant. Both are powerful. Neither replaces the other. Together, they cover your whole workflow.

By the end of this module, you'll be navigating between them fluidly — and the question won't be "can I use Co-pilot for this?" but "which Co-pilot is the right one here?"
