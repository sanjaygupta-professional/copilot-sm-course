# Velocity Squad — Conflict Pattern Map

## Active Tensions (Sprint 47)

### 1. Lisa Chen ↔ Raj Patel: Story Quality
**Surface behavior:** Lisa rewrites acceptance criteria during sprint planning. Raj goes quiet when she does this.
**Frequency:** Every planning session for the last 3 sprints.
**Underlying dynamics:**
- Lisa values technical precision and feels responsible for code quality
- Raj is new to the PO role (6 months) and is still building confidence
- Lisa's corrections, while technically valid, undermine Raj's authority
- Raj interprets feedback as "you're not good enough" rather than "let's improve the story"

**Escalation risk:** Medium-High. If unaddressed, Raj may disengage from writing stories altogether, or Lisa may start bypassing him for requirements.

---

### 2. James Rodriguez ↔ Lisa Chen: Definition of Done
**Surface behavior:** James insists on comprehensive test coverage before marking stories done. Lisa pushes back, saying it slows velocity.
**Frequency:** Comes up in every sprint review when stories are discussed.
**Underlying dynamics:**
- James is a quality advocate (5 years QA experience) — his professional identity is tied to quality
- Lisa is measured (implicitly) on velocity and delivery
- Both are right: quality matters AND speed matters
- The real issue is that the team hasn't agreed on a shared DoD that balances both

**Escalation risk:** Medium. This is a healthy tension if facilitated well. Becomes toxic if it turns personal.

---

### 3. Marcus Williams — Silent in Groups
**Surface behavior:** Marcus rarely speaks in standups, planning, or retros. Defers to Lisa on technical decisions. In 1:1s with the SM, he's insightful and articulate.
**Frequency:** Every group ceremony.
**Underlying dynamics:**
- Marcus is junior (2 years) surrounded by experienced colleagues
- Lisa's strong personality can inadvertently dominate discussions
- Marcus may fear being wrong in front of the group
- Cultural factor: Marcus's communication style is reflective, not performative

**Escalation risk:** Low (no conflict), but High impact on team decisions (missing Marcus's perspective).

---

### 4. Priya Sharma — Visibility Gap
**Surface behavior:** Priya's contributions are undervalued. She's mentioned less in sprint reviews. Her work is attributed to "the team" while co-located members get individual recognition.
**Frequency:** Ongoing, worsening as team becomes more co-located in habits.
**Underlying dynamics:**
- Timezone difference (IST) means Priya misses informal conversations
- She can't do "hallway chats" that build social capital
- Her work quality is high, but it's invisible work (backend, integration)
- Priya has started working weekends to compensate — burnout risk

**Escalation risk:** High. If Priya feels unrecognized, she may disengage or leave.

---

### 5. David Kim — Stretched Thin
**Surface behavior:** David misses design review deadlines. Team expresses frustration. David apologizes but nothing changes.
**Frequency:** 7 occurrences in last 4 sprints.
**Underlying dynamics:**
- David is shared across 3 teams — this is an organizational problem, not David's fault
- Team frustration is directed at David (the person) rather than the system
- David feels guilty and overworks, leading to quality drops
- No one has escalated this as a structural issue

**Escalation risk:** High. David may burn out or leave. Team may develop resentment.

---
