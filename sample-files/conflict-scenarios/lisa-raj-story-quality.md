# Conflict Scenario: Lisa vs. Raj — Story Quality

## Background

**Sprint Planning, Sprint 48.** Raj presents user story AKX-1247:

> "As a user, I want to search for documents so that I can find what I need."

Lisa immediately says: "That's too vague. What kind of search? Full-text? Metadata? What does 'find what I need' even mean? We can't estimate this."

Raj pauses, then says quietly: "I thought we could discuss the details in planning."

Lisa responds: "We shouldn't be writing acceptance criteria during planning. That should come in refined."

The room goes quiet. Marcus looks at his laptop. James shifts uncomfortably.

---

## What's Really Happening

### Lisa's Perspective
- She's seen vague stories lead to rework (Sprint 44: 2 stories re-opened due to unclear requirements)
- She feels responsible for the team's technical quality
- She's frustrated that this pattern keeps repeating
- She doesn't realize her delivery style is damaging Raj's confidence

### Raj's Perspective
- He's 6 months into the PO role — still learning story writing
- He's been improving (Sprint 46 stories were much better)
- He interprets Lisa's feedback as personal criticism, not story feedback
- He's starting to dread planning sessions
- He's considered asking Jennifer for a move to a different team

### The Team's Perspective
- Marcus: Uncomfortable, doesn't want to take sides
- James: Agrees stories need better AC, but dislikes Lisa's approach
- Priya: Watching via video call, feeling disconnected from the tension
- Emma: Too new to understand the dynamic

---

## Facilitation Scenarios

### Round 1: Bad Facilitation (What NOT to Do)
SM says: "Lisa's right, Raj. The stories need better acceptance criteria before planning."
- **Why this fails:** Takes sides. Undermines Raj publicly. Lisa's behavior goes unaddressed.

### Round 2: Adequate Facilitation
SM says: "Good catch on the specificity. Raj, can you walk us through the user's actual workflow? Lisa, what specific criteria would make this estimable?"
- **Why this is okay but not great:** Redirects the conversation productively but doesn't address the pattern or the interpersonal damage.

### Round 3: Excellent Facilitation
SM says: "Let's pause. I'm noticing a pattern I want to name. We have a story that needs more detail — that's a refinement gap, not a planning problem. Raj, let's you and me and Lisa do a 15-minute refinement session after planning to flesh this out. For now, let's park AKX-1247 and move to the next story."

Then, AFTER planning, in a 1:1 with each:
- To Lisa: "Your eye for quality is an asset. But when you critique stories in front of the team, Raj hears 'you failed' not 'let's improve this.' Can we find a way to give this feedback in refinement instead?"
- To Raj: "Your stories have been improving — Sprint 46 was your best yet. Lisa's feedback is about the story, not about you. But I'll work with her on the timing and delivery."

---

## Success Criteria

After intervention, look for:
- [ ] Lisa gives story feedback in refinement, not planning (behavior change)
- [ ] Raj's story quality continues improving (not regressing due to fear)
- [ ] Planning sessions feel psychologically safe for all participants
- [ ] Neither Lisa nor Raj feels the SM took the other's side
- [ ] The pattern doesn't resurface for 2+ sprints

## Regression Warning Signs
- Raj stops writing detailed acceptance criteria (giving up)
- Lisa starts going directly to developers with requirements (bypassing Raj)
- Planning sessions get shorter because fewer people speak up
- Raj asks to change teams or roles
