# AI-Enabled Scrum Master Competency Model

**Purpose:** Self-assessment framework for Scrum Masters building AI-augmented practices
**Version:** 1.0
**Aligned To:** Copilot for Scrum Masters course levels

---

## Overview

This model defines 6 competency areas across 5 proficiency levels. Each competency maps to course modules where those skills are developed.

| Level | Label | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Novice | Knows the concept; needs guidance to apply it |
| 2 | Practitioner | Can apply it independently in standard situations |
| 3 | Skilled | Applies it consistently; adapts to context |
| 4 | Advanced | Teaches others; handles complex/novel situations |
| 5 | Expert | Innovates; shapes organizational practice |

---

## Competency 1: AI Tool Proficiency

**What it is:** Ability to use Microsoft Copilot (and adjacent AI tools) effectively for SM tasks — prompt engineering, output evaluation, and workflow integration.

**Course alignment:** Level 1 (Foundation modules 1.1-1.4)

| Proficiency | Descriptor |
|-------------|------------|
| **Beginner (1-2)** | Can write basic prompts and get useful output. Understands that prompt structure affects output quality. Uses Copilot for simple tasks like summarizing text or drafting messages. Occasionally gets poor results and isn't sure why. |
| **Intermediate (3)** | Writes structured prompts with role, context, and format specifications. Iterates on prompts to improve output. Has a personal library of 10+ tested prompts. Can explain to others why a prompt works or doesn't. Routinely uses Copilot in Teams, Word, and Excel. |
| **Advanced (4-5)** | Designs prompt chains for multi-step workflows. Creates reusable prompt templates for the SM community. Evaluates new AI features and determines SM relevance. Understands token limits, context windows, and model behavior. Contributes to organizational AI adoption strategy. |

---

## Competency 2: Advanced Facilitation with AI

**What it is:** Using AI to prepare, run, and follow up on Scrum ceremonies and team conversations — without letting AI replace the human facilitation skills.

**Course alignment:** Level 2 (modules 2.1-2.4)

| Proficiency | Descriptor |
|-------------|------------|
| **Beginner (1-2)** | Uses Copilot to generate meeting agendas and take notes. Can process raw ceremony notes into action items. Relies on AI for structure but facilitates the conversation personally. Sometimes over-depends on AI-generated questions. |
| **Intermediate (3)** | Runs a full ceremony chain: AI-assisted prep → human facilitation → AI-assisted processing → AI-assisted follow-up. Adapts AI-generated questions based on room energy. Uses retrospective data analysis to surface non-obvious patterns. Balances AI efficiency with psychological safety. |
| **Advanced (4-5)** | Designs novel ceremony formats using AI as a real-time facilitation partner. Uses AI to detect facilitation anti-patterns in their own practice. Creates ceremony templates that other SMs adopt. Can facilitate effectively when AI tools are unavailable — AI augments, never replaces. |

---

## Competency 3: Data-Driven Decision Making

**What it is:** Leveraging sprint data, team health metrics, and trend analysis (via AI) to make informed decisions and surface insights the team wouldn't see otherwise.

**Course alignment:** Level 4 (modules 4.1-4.3)

| Proficiency | Descriptor |
|-------------|------------|
| **Beginner (1-2)** | Tracks basic metrics (velocity, burndown) and can ask Copilot to summarize trends. Understands that data should inform but not dictate decisions. Can generate a sprint report with AI assistance. |
| **Intermediate (3)** | Builds early warning prompts that detect sprint risks from daily signals. Combines multiple data sources (standup notes, burndown, impediment log) into integrated analysis. Uses historical sprint data to improve planning accuracy. Presents data insights to the team in accessible, non-threatening ways. |
| **Advanced (4-5)** | Creates predictive models for sprint outcomes using prompt-based analysis. Identifies systemic patterns across multiple sprints that require organizational intervention. Coaches POs on data-driven backlog prioritization. Distinguishes between correlation and causation in team metrics — uses data responsibly. |

---

## Competency 4: Scaling & Influence

**What it is:** Using data and structured communication to influence organizational decisions, resolve cross-team impediments, and operate at the program/portfolio level.

**Course alignment:** Level 5 (modules 5.1-5.3)

| Proficiency | Descriptor |
|-------------|------------|
| **Beginner (1-2)** | Can document organizational impediments and present them to leadership. Uses Copilot to draft business cases and stakeholder communications. Understands the difference between team-level and organizational-level problems. |
| **Intermediate (3)** | Maps stakeholder influence networks and designs engagement strategies. Builds data-driven business cases that quantify the cost of organizational impediments. Uses AI to prepare executive pitches with anticipated objections. Successfully navigates one organizational impediment to resolution. |
| **Advanced (4-5)** | Operates as an RTE or program-level coordinator. Designs cross-team coordination mechanisms. Influences organizational design decisions (team topology, resource allocation). Mentors other SMs in stakeholder management. Uses AI to maintain program-level visibility across multiple teams. |

---

## Competency 5: AI Coaching & Automation

**What it is:** Using AI as a coaching tool for self-improvement and team development — reflective practice, scenario simulation, and continuous learning.

**Course alignment:** Level 6 (modules 6.1-6.3)

| Proficiency | Descriptor |
|-------------|------------|
| **Beginner (1-2)** | Uses Copilot for basic self-reflection ("What went well today?"). Can simulate simple coaching conversations with AI. Maintains an irregular reflective journal. Understands the concept of a "coaching gym" but hasn't established a regular practice. |
| **Intermediate (3)** | Maintains a weekly reflective journal with AI-assisted pattern analysis. Runs regular coaching gym sessions (weekly). Uses AI to prep for difficult conversations with specific team members. Has a personal AI SM operating system with established weekly rhythm. Identifies 3+ hours/week of time savings from AI automation. |
| **Advanced (4-5)** | Designs coaching programs for other SMs incorporating AI tools. Creates custom coaching scenarios based on real team dynamics. Uses AI to analyze their own facilitation patterns and improve over time. Contributes to the organization's AI adoption strategy for agile roles. Balances AI assistance with authentic human connection — knows when NOT to use AI. |

---

## Competency 6: Responsible AI Leadership

**What it is:** Applying ethical judgment, maintaining transparency, ensuring fairness, and modeling responsible AI use for the team and organization.

**Course alignment:** Level 2+ (integrated across all modules)

| Proficiency | Descriptor |
|-------------|------------|
| **Beginner (1-2)** | Understands basic AI ethics: bias, hallucination, privacy. Discloses when content is AI-generated. Reviews AI output before sharing with stakeholders. Doesn't use AI to make decisions about individual performance. |
| **Intermediate (3)** | Has a personal AI ethics framework (what they will and won't use AI for). Educates the team on responsible AI use in ceremonies. Identifies potential bias in AI-generated analysis (e.g., velocity comparisons between team members). Maintains human judgment as the final decision-maker. Handles data privacy appropriately — knows what NOT to put into AI tools. |
| **Advanced (4-5)** | Shapes organizational AI governance policies for agile teams. Facilitates team discussions on AI ethics and boundaries. Identifies systemic bias risks in AI-assisted processes. Advocates for transparency in AI use at the program level. Models the principle: "AI augments human judgment; it never replaces it." |
