# Quarterly SM Update for Executives

A one-page template for communicating Scrum Master impact to senior leadership. Written in business language — no agile jargon.

---

## Formatting Guidance

- **One page maximum.** If it doesn't fit on one page, cut it. Executives scan; they don't read.
- **Lead with outcomes, not activities.** "Delivery predictability improved 25%" not "I facilitated 26 retrospectives."
- **Business language only.** Say "delivery cycle" not "sprint." Say "team output" not "velocity." Say "commitment reliability" not "sprint goal achievement."
- **Numbers first, narrative second.** Every section should open with a metric or result.
- **No agile jargon.** If your VP would need to Google a term, replace it.

### Jargon Translation Guide

| Agile Term | Executive-Friendly Alternative |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| Sprint | Delivery cycle (2-week period) |
| Velocity | Team output / throughput |
| Sprint goal achievement | Commitment reliability |
| Story points | Work units |
| Impediment | Blocker / delivery risk |
| Retrospective | Team improvement review |
| Standup | Daily coordination check-in |
| Backlog | Prioritized work pipeline |
| Cycle time | Time from start to delivery |
| WIP limit | Focus management / parallel work cap |
| Carryover | Incomplete commitments |
| Burndown | Progress tracking |

# Example: Velocity Squad Q3 2025 Update

---

**QUARTERLY TEAM UPDATE**

**Team:** Velocity Squad — AKX Platform (Accenture Knowledge Exchange)
**Period:** Q3 2025 (July - September, Sprints 40-47)
**Prepared by:** [Your Name], Scrum Master
**Date:** October 3, 2025

---

### Executive Summary

The Velocity Squad improved commitment reliability from 60% to 85% while maintaining consistent output of ~48 work units per delivery cycle. The biggest driver was reducing time lost to unresolved blockers, which recovered an estimated 88 developer-days over the quarter. Next quarter, we are focused on reducing time-to-delivery by 20% through tighter cross-team dependency management.

---

### Delivery Metrics

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q3 2025 | Change |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Team Output** (avg work units per cycle) | 46 | 48 | +4% |
| **Commitment Reliability** | 60% | 85% | +25 ppt |
| **Time to Delivery** (avg days, start to done) | 6.2 days | 4.1 days | -34% |
| **Incomplete Commitments** | 22% of work | 8% of work | -14 ppt |
| **Production Issues** (per delivery cycle) | 3.5 | 1.8 | -49% |

---

### Team Health

Based on anonymous team surveys conducted at the end of each delivery cycle.

| Dimension | Rating (1-5) | Trend | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|-------|
| Collaboration | 4.1 | Up | Strongest improvement area; cross-role pairing increased |
| Technical Confidence | 3.5 | Stable | Architecture complexity remains a concern |
| Workload Balance | 3.6 | Up | Better commitment sizing reduced overtime |
| Clarity of Direction | 3.9 | Up | Product Owner coaching improved requirement quality |
| Engagement | 4.0 | Up | Team morale noticeably improved since August |

**Overall Team Health: 3.8 / 5** (up from 3.1 at start of quarter)

---

### Key Wins This Quarter

1. **Blocker resolution time cut by 60%** — Implemented a structured escalation process that reduced average resolution from 5 days to 2 days, recovering approximately 88 developer-days of productive time over the quarter.

2. **Commitment reliability reached 85%** — Coaching the Product Owner on capacity-based planning and introducing team confidence checks during planning sessions. Stakeholders can now plan downstream work around our commitments with confidence.

3. **Daily coordination meetings reduced by 44%** — Restructured the format to stay within 15 minutes while improving information flow. The team recovered approximately 76 hours of meeting time over the quarter.

---

### Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation | Status |
|------|--------|------------|--------|
| David Kim (UX) shared across 3 teams | Design bottleneck delays 2-3 items per cycle; adds 1-2 days to delivery time | Working with resource management to increase David's allocation from 40% to 60%; building team capability to handle basic UX tasks | Needs support |
| Priya Sharma's time zone offset (Bangalore) | Limits real-time collaboration to 3-hour overlap window; slows code review turnaround | Established async handoff protocol; shifted one weekly sync to accommodate IST | On track |
| AKX platform migration planned for Q4 | Will consume significant team capacity and introduce technical risk during transition | Pre-planning sessions with Infrastructure Lead started; requesting dedicated migration support | Needs support |

---

### Next Quarter Focus

1. **Reduce time-to-delivery by 20%** — Target average of 3.3 days (down from 4.1) by implementing parallel work management and earlier cross-team dependency identification. Expected by end of Sprint 53.

2. **Increase UX design capacity** — Resolve the David Kim bottleneck through either increased allocation or team skill development. Goal: zero design-blocked items by Sprint 51.

3. **Prepare for AKX platform migration** — Establish technical readiness and risk mitigation plan by Sprint 50 so the migration does not disrupt ongoing delivery commitments.
