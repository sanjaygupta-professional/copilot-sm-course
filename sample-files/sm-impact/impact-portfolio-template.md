## Key Interventions

### Intervention 1: Standup Reset

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Problem Observed** | Standups ran 25+ minutes. Discussions turned into problem-solving sessions. Priya (Bangalore) was disengaged due to time zone fatigue. |
| **What You Did** | Introduced a strict 15-minute timebox with a visible timer. Created a "parking lot" board for follow-up discussions. Moved detailed blocker conversations to 10-minute after-standup huddles with only relevant people. |
| **What Changed** | Standups consistently under 15 minutes. Priya started contributing more because meetings ended on time. The team began self-policing discussions that went off-topic. |
| **Quantified Impact** | 10 min saved per standup x 10 standups/sprint x 7 team members = 700 person-minutes (~11.7 hours) recovered per sprint. |
| **Evidence** | Meeting duration logs, Priya's retro feedback ("I finally feel like standup respects my time"), team health survey improvement in "Meetings" category. |

### Intervention 2: Impediment Escalation Framework

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Problem Observed** | Blockers sat unresolved for 5+ days. No clear escalation path. Marcus and Emma would wait silently rather than raise issues. The team lost an average of 8 story points per sprint to unresolved blockers. |
| **What You Did** | Created a 3-tier escalation model: (1) team-level, resolve within 1 day; (2) SM-level, escalate to Tom Harrison or cross-team contacts within 2 days; (3) leadership-level, escalate to Jennifer Walsh if unresolved after 3 days. Set up a shared blocker board visible to the team. |
| **What Changed** | Average resolution dropped from 5.2 to 2.1 days. Marcus started raising blockers same-day. The team recovered approximately 5 story points per sprint previously lost to blocked work. |
| **Quantified Impact** | 5 points recovered/sprint x 8 sprints = 40 additional story points delivered over the period. Resolution time reduced by 60%. |
| **Evidence** | Jira blocker log with timestamps, velocity recovery trend from Sprint 44 onward, Marcus's feedback in 1:1. |

### Intervention 3: PO Coaching on Sprint Commitment

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Problem Observed** | Raj consistently pushed the team to overcommit. Sprint goal achievement was at 60%. The team was carrying over 22% of stories, which hurt morale and stakeholder trust. |
| **What You Did** | Started weekly 30-minute PO coaching sessions with Raj. Introduced capacity-based planning using historical velocity data. Created a visual "commitment confidence" check during planning where each team member rates their confidence (1-5) on the proposed sprint scope. |
| **What Changed** | Raj began proposing scope within the team's capacity range. Sprint goal achievement rose from 60% to 85%. Carryover dropped from 22% to 8%. Raj reported feeling "less stressed" about sprint reviews. |
| **Quantified Impact** | Sprint goal achievement +25 percentage points. Carryover reduced by 14 percentage points. Estimated 3 hours saved per sprint in re-planning and stakeholder expectation management. |
| **Evidence** | Sprint planning velocity vs. commitment data, Raj's self-assessment, Jennifer Walsh's comment in Scrum of Scrums: "The Velocity Squad's delivery has become much more predictable." |
