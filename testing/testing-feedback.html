Feedback & Quality Gates

Every piece of feedback passes through two validation checkpoints before reaching the course author. This process was proven in the PM course alpha test (170 findings → 130 actionable → 57 implementation items after deduplication).

Checkpoint 1: Team Lead Review

The team lead evaluates each feedback item against these criteria:

CriteriaPassFail (return to tester)
SpecificReferences exact module, section, exercise, or prompt“The course is confusing” or “Level 4 needs work”
Observation + ExpectationBoth fields filled with concrete detailVague or missing either field
Non-duplicateNot already submitted by another team memberRepeat of existing finding (merge with original)
Right lensesTagged lenses actually match the findingPrompt issue tagged as UX only; coaching concern tagged as content accuracy
ActionableThe course author could fix this with a specific change“Copilot doesn’t always give good answers” (platform limitation, not course issue)

Lead actions:

  • Approve — Forward to cross-team validation
  • Return — Send back with a note on what to clarify (tester revises and resubmits)
  • Merge — Combine with an existing finding that covers the same issue

Checkpoint 2: Cross-Team Lead Validation

A lead from a different team reviews approved feedback:

They check forWhy
AccuracyDoes the finding hold up? Is the “bug” actually a bug, or a design choice?
RelevanceIs this about the course, or about Copilot’s general limitations?
ProportionalityDoes the severity match the impact? A typo isn’t Critical.
Cross-team contextDid another team already find the same thing? Does a different lens add nuance?

Feedback Template

One item per finding. Keep it simple — say what you saw, what you expected, and which lenses it touches.

## [Module ID] — [Short title]

**Observation:** [What you saw or experienced — be specific.
  Quote the text, name the exercise, describe the step.]

**Expectation:** [What should have happened instead]

**Lenses:** [L1, L7] ← tag 1–3 lenses this relates to

That’s it. No severity ratings from testers, no type checkboxes, no lengthy evidence sections. The course author triages priority. Your job is to notice and describe — clearly and specifically.

Good Example

## 2.1 — Ceremony facilitation prompt produces generic agenda

**Observation:** Exercise 2 asks us to generate a Sprint Review agenda
  using the prompt on page 3. The output is a generic meeting agenda
  (Welcome, Review, Demo, Feedback, Close) with no Velocity Squad
  context, despite uploading sprint-review-raw.md as instructed.

**Expectation:** Output should reference actual Sprint 47 items,
  team members, and demo-ready features from the uploaded file.

**Lenses:** [L3, L4]

Bad Example (would be returned)

## Level 2 — Needs improvement

**Observation:** The prompts in Level 2 don't always work well.

**Expectation:** They should work better.

**Lenses:** [L3]

This would be returned by the team lead: no specific module, no specific prompt, no description of what “doesn’t work well” means, and no expected vs actual comparison.

Severity Guidelines

Testers don’t assign severity — the course author does during triage. But understanding the scale helps calibrate what’s worth reporting:

Critical

Blocks learning. Prompt crashes, exercise can’t be completed, factually wrong Scrum guidance, broken navigation preventing access to modules.

Major

Significantly impairs learning. Prompt produces misleading output, exercise success criteria unclear, inconsistent data across modules, key concept unexplained.

Moderate

Noticeable quality issue. Cognitive overload in a section, unclear transition between topics, callout box mislabeled, dark mode renders text unreadable in one area.

Minor

Polish. Typos, inconsistent formatting, copy button has wrong hover state, TOC highlights wrong section briefly during fast scroll.

Consolidated Report Format

The Meta-Team (4 leads + course author) delivers one final report.

1. Executive Summary

  • Total findings: submitted → approved → endorsed (funnel)
  • Breakdown by lens (which lenses surfaced the most issues?)
  • Breakdown by level (which levels need the most work?)
  • Top 3 cross-cutting themes

2. Findings by Module

All endorsed findings grouped by module (1.1 through 6.3), with lens tags. This is the primary view for implementation.

3. Findings by Lens

Same findings regrouped by lens — reveals systemic patterns. Example: if L7 (Scrum Fidelity) flags issues in 8 of 27 modules, there’s a systemic terminology problem.

4. Cross-Team Agreement

Findings where multiple teams independently flagged the same issue — high-confidence problems. These get highest implementation priority.

5. Positive Findings

What works well, organized by lens. Protect these during revisions. In the PM course, Team 2 specifically praised the progressive scaffolding in modules 1.3 and 2.1 — this kind of signal prevents regression.
