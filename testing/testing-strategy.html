Why This Testing Strategy Exists

The SM course is nearly 2x the scale of the PM course (27 modules vs 15, 6 levels vs 3, ~13h vs ~6h). More importantly, it teaches a fundamentally different skill set. Product Managers use Copilot as a productivity tool. Scrum Masters use it as a coaching partner, facilitation aide, and analytical lens — augmenting human judgment, not replacing tasks.

This means testing must evaluate not just "does the prompt work?" but "does the course actually develop practitioner capability?"

Scale Comparison: PM vs SM Course

PM Course (completed)

  • 15 modules across 3 levels
  • ~6 hours total duration
  • 22 resource files
  • Focus: tool productivity
  • Context: Product Manager at Accenture
  • Static site: basic HTML, no interactivity

SM Course (this test cycle)

  • 27 modules across 6 levels
  • ~13 hours total duration
  • 78 resource files
  • Focus: coaching + facilitation + analytics
  • Context: Scrum Master → AI-native practitioner
  • Static site: 14 interactive features

Guiding Principles

PrincipleWhy it matters for the SM course
Every team, every moduleAll 4 teams test all 27 modules — different practitioner backgrounds catch different issues. An RTE spots scaling gaps a team-level SM misses.
Focus more, not onlyEach team applies all 10 lenses but goes deeper on their 2 owned lenses. The owned team is the authoritative voice.
Test like a learner, think like a coachExperience the course as a Scrum Master encountering AI for the first time, then evaluate with your practitioner expertise. The SM audience is sophisticated — they'll notice hand-waving.
Quality over volume10 validated, actionable findings beat 50 vague complaints. The PM course test generated 170 findings but only 57 survived deduplication. Be specific from the start.
Two-gate validationNo feedback reaches the course author without review by own lead + a second team's lead. Prevents opinion-as-finding and ensures cross-team calibration.
Practitioner judgment, not just tool testingUnlike the PM course, many SM modules teach human skills augmented by AI (coaching, facilitation, conflict resolution). Evaluate whether the course develops real capability, not just Copilot button-pressing.

The 10 Testing Lenses — Overview

Every module is evaluated against 10 lenses. Two are common (applied by all teams with equal rigor). Eight are team-owned (2 per team — these teams go deeper and are the authoritative voice).

L1: Content Accuracy L2: Learner Clarity L3: Prompt Effectiveness L4: Exercise Quality L5: Instructional Design L6: Progressive Flow L7: Scrum Fidelity & Coaching Depth L8: Practitioner Applicability L9: Platform & Interactive Features L10: Gaps & Completeness
TeamIdentityOwned Lenses
Team 1“Does it work?”L3: Prompt Effectiveness · L4: Exercise Quality
Team 2“Does it teach?”L5: Instructional Design · L6: Progressive Flow
Team 3“Does it develop practitioners?”L7: Scrum Fidelity & Coaching Depth · L8: Practitioner Applicability
Team 4“Does it deliver?”L9: Platform & Interactive Features · L10: Gaps & Completeness

Team 3's identity — “Does it develop practitioners?” — is new compared to the PM course's “Does it matter?”. This reflects the SM course's emphasis on building coaching, facilitation, and conflict resolution skills beyond AI tool usage.
