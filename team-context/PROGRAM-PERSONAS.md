## Ana Martinez - Catalyst Crew Scrum Master

### Background

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | Scrum Master, Catalyst Crew |
| **Age** | 36 |
| **Location** | New York, NY |
| **Experience** | 7 years as Scrum Master |
| **Certifications** | PSM-II, SAFe Agilist |
| **Career Goal** | Transition to Agile Coach role |
| **Relationship to You** | Closest SM peer, weekly coffee meetings |

### Professional Profile

Ana is the most experienced SM in the Horizon Program. She has deep facilitation skills and a genuine talent for creating psychological safety within her team. Her ceremonies are well-run, her team trusts her, and she consistently gets positive feedback from team members.

Her blind spot is executive communication. Ana operates brilliantly at the team level but avoids escalating to Jennifer Walsh, even when program-level support is needed. She views asking for help as a sign of weakness, which limits her effectiveness when problems exceed what she can solve within her team.

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional facilitation skills
- Creates psychological safety naturally
- Strong coaching instincts with team members
- Deep knowledge of agile frameworks
- Empathetic listener

**Growth Areas:**
- Executive communication and stakeholder management
- Building data-driven business cases
- Escalating issues before they become crises
- Strategic thinking beyond the team level
- Saying difficult truths to leadership

### Your Relationship

You and Ana meet for coffee every Wednesday morning. These conversations are the backbone of your peer support network. She asks your advice on the Derek/Sofia tech debt conflict. You ask her advice on coaching Raj through his PO growing pains. Neither of you has all the answers, but you think better together.

Ana would be an excellent Agile Coach but needs to develop her executive presence. Several course exercises involve helping Ana prepare for leadership conversations using Copilot.

## Chris Wong - Nexus Team Scrum Master

### Background

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | Scrum Master, Nexus Team |
| **Age** | 31 |
| **Location** | San Francisco, CA |
| **Experience** | 4 years as Scrum Master |
| **Previous Career** | Software Developer (5 years) |
| **Certifications** | PSM-I, CSM |
| **Relationship to You** | Mentee, newer SM you informally guide |

### Professional Profile

Chris transitioned from software development to Scrum Mastering 4 years ago. He brings valuable technical empathy to the role - he understands what developers go through and can spot technical risks early. However, his developer instincts sometimes work against him as an SM.

When a problem comes up in a standup, Chris's first instinct is to solve it. He'll sketch a solution, suggest an approach, or even offer to pair on the code. This makes the team depend on him for technical direction rather than developing their own problem-solving capabilities.

The Nexus Team is his first distributed team, and the timezone and cultural complexity is stretching him. He's eager to learn but doesn't always know what to ask.

**Strengths:**
- Technical empathy and awareness
- Quick learner, receptive to feedback
- Energetic and committed
- Good rapport with developers
- Transparent about what he doesn't know

**Growth Areas:**
- Coaching vs. solving (letting the team struggle productively)
- Distributed team facilitation
- Cross-cultural communication awareness
- Having difficult conversations (avoids confrontation)
- Building team norms instead of dictating them

### Your Relationship

Chris looks up to you as a more experienced SM. He messages you on Teams when he's stuck, usually with questions like "How would you handle this?" You try to coach him rather than give direct answers, but sometimes he just needs a sounding board.

He's particularly struggling with Ben Cooper dominating meetings while Hiroshi Tanaka stays silent. He knows it's a problem but doesn't know how to address it without making Ben feel criticized.

## Jennifer Walsh - VP Internal Digital Products / RTE

### Background

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | VP Internal Digital Products, RTE-equivalent for Horizon Program |
| **Age** | 47 |
| **Location** | New York, NY |
| **Tenure** | 15 years at Accenture |
| **Reports to** | Richard Thorne (CKO) |
| **Direct Reports** | 4 SMs (including you), PO leads |
| **Relationship to You** | Your manager, weekly 1:1 |

### Professional Profile

Jennifer is a data-driven leader who has spent 15 years at Accenture rising through the ranks. She respects competence, preparation, and directness. She does not respond well to vague concerns or anecdotal evidence.

Her management style is hands-off with people she trusts and hands-on with people she doesn't. You've earned her trust through consistent delivery and clear communication. She gives you the hardest problems (like interim-covering Insight Engine) because she knows you'll tell her the truth about what you find.

**What She Values:**
- Data over opinions ("Show me the trend, not the feeling")
- Preparation (come to 1:1s with an agenda and specific asks)
- Ownership (flag problems early, bring proposed solutions)
- Efficiency (she has back-to-back meetings; don't waste her time)
- Honesty (she'd rather hear bad news early than be surprised)

**Her Question:**

Jennifer regularly asks: "What would happen if we didn't have a Scrum Master?" She's not threatening your role. She genuinely wants to understand and articulate the value of SMs to Richard Thorne and the broader organization. Your ability to answer this question with evidence is one of the undercurrents of the course.

### Your 1:1 Dynamic

Weekly, Tuesdays at 4:00 PM ET, 30 minutes. Jennifer expects:

1. **Team status** (2 minutes) - Green/yellow/red with one sentence of context
2. **Metrics update** (5 minutes) - What moved, what didn't, why
3. **Blockers needing her help** (5 minutes) - Specific, actionable asks
4. **Program observations** (5 minutes) - Cross-team issues you've noticed
5. **Your development** (5 minutes) - What you're learning, where you need support

She'll cancel other meetings to keep yours. That's a sign of respect - don't waste it.

## Richard Thorne - Chief Knowledge Officer

### Background

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | Chief Knowledge Officer, Executive Sponsor |
| **Age** | 54 |
| **Location** | New York, NY |
| **Tenure** | 20 years at Accenture |
| **Scope** | All knowledge management initiatives globally |
| **Relationship to You** | Executive sponsor, quarterly presentations |

### Professional Profile

Richard is the executive sponsor of the AKX platform. He cares about business outcomes: adoption rates, time-to-knowledge, competitive advantage through knowledge sharing. He does not care about sprint velocity, story points, or agile mechanics.

When you present to Richard, you have 5 minutes. He will ask 2-3 sharp questions. If you don't have the data, he'll move on and your credibility drops. If you have the data and a clear narrative, he'll remember you.

**What He Cares About:**
- OKR alignment (are teams working on what matters?)
- Adoption metrics (are people using the platform?)
- ROI (is the investment in AKX paying off?)
- Competitive positioning (how does AKX compare to industry tools?)
- Speed to market (are teams delivering fast enough?)

**What He Does Not Care About:**
- Sprint mechanics or agile process details
- Team-level problems (unless they threaten delivery)
- Ceremony design or facilitation techniques
- How many story points were completed

### Presenting to Richard

You present to Richard once per quarter at the PI boundary. Jennifer coaches you on these presentations. The format:

1. **Business outcome progress** (2 minutes) - OKR status, adoption numbers
2. **Key achievements** (1 minute) - Top 3 deliverables with business impact
3. **Risks and mitigation** (1 minute) - What could go wrong, what you're doing about it
4. **Ask** (1 minute) - What you need from him (decisions, resources, air cover)

Several course exercises involve using Copilot to prepare executive-level summaries from team-level data.

## Tom Harrison - Infrastructure Lead

### Background

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | Infrastructure Lead |
| **Age** | 41 |
| **Location** | New York, NY |
| **Team Size** | 2 (Tom + one junior engineer) |
| **Scope** | Infrastructure and DevOps for all 4 AKX teams |
| **Relationship to You** | Shared dependency, frequent collaboration |

### Professional Profile

Tom is one of the most competent people in the Horizon Program and also one of the most overworked. His team of 2 supports infrastructure for all 4 AKX teams, which means he's constantly context-switching and triaging requests.

Tom is helpful, knowledgeable, and genuinely wants to support every team. The problem is math: there are not enough hours in the day. His average response time for infrastructure requests is 5 business days, which creates bottlenecks across the program.

**Key Facts:**
- His team should be 4-5 people; it's 2
- He works 55-60 hours per week consistently
- He prioritizes by urgency, not by program priority, because everything feels urgent
- Jennifer knows his team is understaffed but hasn't secured headcount approval yet
- He attends SoS but often has to leave early for firefighting

**Working with Tom:**
- Be specific in your requests (he hates vague "we need infrastructure help")
- Give him lead time (2 weeks minimum for non-urgent requests)
- Batch requests when possible (one conversation > five Slack messages)
- Advocate for his team's staffing in your 1:1 with Jennifer

## Sarah Mitchell - Managing Director, Strategy

### Background

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | Managing Director, Strategy |
| **Age** | 49 |
| **Location** | New York, NY |
| **Scope** | Organizational digital transformation metrics |
| **Relationship to You** | Stakeholder, occasional interaction |

### Professional Profile

Sarah drives organizational adoption metrics for Accenture's internal digital transformation initiatives. She sits outside the AKX delivery organization but has significant influence over funding and strategic direction.

Sarah is currently interested in whether AI tools (including Microsoft Copilot) are making teams more productive. She's been tracking industry trends and wants internal case studies. Your work with Copilot as an SM tool could become one of those case studies.

**What She Wants to Know:**
- Are AI tools making Scrum Masters more effective? How would you measure that?
- Can AI-assisted facilitation improve ceremony outcomes?
- What does "SM productivity" even mean, and can it be quantified?
- Are there organizational scaling opportunities if the pilot succeeds?

**Why This Matters:**

If Sarah sees positive results from your Copilot usage, she could advocate for:
- Copilot licenses for all SMs across Accenture
- A formal pilot program with measurement framework
- Your involvement as a subject matter expert in the rollout
- Budget for training and enablement

This is background context that adds stakes to the course exercises. You're not just learning Copilot for yourself - you might be building the case for organizational adoption.
