# Difficult Conversation Preparation Template

## When to Use
Use before any conversation you're nervous about — coaching, conflict mediation, escalation, performance feedback, or delivering bad news.

## The Preparation Prompt

```
Help me prepare for a difficult conversation.

THE SITUATION:
- Who: [person's name and role]
- What: [topic of the conversation]
- Why now: [what triggered this conversation]
- Our relationship: [history, trust level, past interactions]
- Stakes: [what happens if this goes well / poorly]

THE PERSON'S LIKELY PERSPECTIVE:
- They probably think: [their likely view of the situation]
- They probably feel: [their emotional state]
- They might be defensive about: [sensitive areas]
- Their underlying need: [what they really want, beneath the surface]

MY GOAL:
- Ideal outcome: [what success looks like]
- Minimum acceptable outcome: [the floor]
- Non-negotiable: [what I won't compromise on]

Please generate:
1. OPENING (2-3 sentences): How to start the conversation. Set a collaborative tone. Avoid blame language.
2. KEY QUESTIONS (3-5): Open questions that help them share their perspective. Sequence from easy to hard.
3. LISTENING FOR: What signals to watch for in their responses. Body language and tone cues.
4. WHAT TO AVOID: Phrases or approaches that could derail the conversation.
5. IF THEY GET DEFENSIVE: De-escalation language for 2-3 likely defensive reactions.
6. CLOSING: How to end the conversation with clear next steps and preserved relationship.
7. FOLLOW-UP: What to do in the 48 hours after the conversation.
```

## Example: Preparing for Lisa Feedback Conversation

```
Help me prepare for a difficult conversation.

THE SITUATION:
- Who: Lisa Chen, Senior Developer and Tech Lead
- What: Her communication style is undermining Raj's confidence as PO
- Why now: It happened again in Sprint 47 planning — she rewrote his stories publicly
- Our relationship: Good. She respects me. I've given her positive feedback before.
- Stakes: If this goes well, Lisa adjusts her approach and Raj grows. If poorly, Lisa feels attacked and our relationship suffers.

THE PERSON'S LIKELY PERSPECTIVE:
- She thinks she's helping the team by ensuring story quality
- She feels responsible for technical excellence
- She might be defensive about being told her "help" is harmful
- Her underlying need: to feel her expertise is valued and the team ships quality work

MY GOAL:
- Ideal outcome: Lisa agrees to give story feedback in refinement, not planning
- Minimum: She acknowledges the impact on Raj
- Non-negotiable: This pattern must change; it's damaging the PO role

Please generate the full preparation guide.
```
