## Introduction

You've learned the tools. You've practiced the skills. Now you need a system.

The difference between Scrum Masters who transform their practice with AI and those who try it once and drift back to old habits comes down to one thing: a repeatable operating system. Not a collection of prompts — a rhythm. A set of habits wired into your actual weekly schedule, with clear triggers, outputs, and feedback loops.

This module is different from the others. The exercises here don't produce one-time outputs — they produce the scaffolding for how you'll work from now on. You'll design your personal system, plan your adoption trajectory, assess your gaps, and — in the final exercise — reflect on how far you've come.

This is the capstone of the course. Take it seriously. What you build here is what you'll actually use on Monday morning.

## Exercise 1: Your AI-SM Operating System (8 min)

**Level: Basic**

An operating system isn't a tool — it's a rhythm. This exercise designs your weekly cadence with specific AI-assisted activities mapped to specific days and time slots.

```
Help me design my weekly AI-augmented Scrum Master operating system.

CONTEXT:
- I'm the SM for the Velocity Squad (7 members) at Accenture
- Sprint 47, Horizon Program (4 teams)
- I also participate in Scrum of Scrums (Monday) and a monthly SM Community of Practice
- My biggest time-sinks (from Module 6.1 audit): meeting docs, sprint reports, blocker tracking
- I've completed all course modules and want to integrate everything I've learned

DESIGN MY WEEKLY RHYTHM:

**MONDAY — Week Kickoff & SoS Prep**
- Allocated AI time: 30 minutes
- Needs: SoS prep (cross-team dependencies, blockers to raise), week planning

**DAILY — Signal Scan & Standup Support**
- Allocated AI time: 10 minutes (before standup)
- Needs: Board scan, anomaly detection, standup prep notes

**WEDNESDAY — Ceremony Prep & Mid-Sprint Check**
- Allocated AI time: 20 minutes
- Needs: Refinement/planning/retro prep (whichever is upcoming), mid-sprint health check

**FRIDAY — Reflection & Report**
- Allocated AI time: 20 minutes
- Needs: Reflective journal (Module 6.2), stakeholder report draft, next week planning

**MONTHLY — Practice Development**
- Allocated AI time: 60 minutes
- Needs: Coaching gym session, edge case library update, skill gap review

For each time slot, provide:
1. The specific prompt or prompt chain to use (reference course modules)
2. Expected output and how to use it
3. Time estimate for the AI interaction vs. human review
4. What to do with the output (where it goes, who sees it)
5. How to tell if this step is actually saving time

Also design:
- **QUARTERLY REVIEW** — How to assess whether the system is working
- **ADJUSTMENT PROTOCOL** — When and how to modify the rhythm
- **FAILURE MODE** — What to do when you skip a day (don't let it cascade)

Format as a one-page reference I can pin next to my monitor.
```

### What You Should See

A concrete weekly schedule, not abstract advice:
- Monday 8:30 AM: Open Copilot, paste SoS prep prompt with this week's cross-team data. Review output in 5 min. Post summary in #scrum-of-scrums channel.
- Daily 9:00 AM: Run signal scan prompt against yesterday's board changes. Flag anything for standup. 3 min AI, 2 min review.
- Wednesday 2:00 PM: Run ceremony prep chain (Module 6.1, Exercise 3). Generate agenda + notes template. 8 min AI, 7 min review and customize.
- Friday 4:00 PM: Reflective journal prompt (Module 6.2, Exercise 5) + sprint report prompt (Module 6.1, Exercise 2). 10 min AI, 10 min review.

The quarterly review should include: hours saved per week (target: 4-6), quality of outputs (are stakeholders noticing?), whether you're spending saved time on coaching (the real goal).

## Exercise 2: The 90-Day Plan (5 min)

**Level: Basic**

Behavior change fails when you try to adopt everything at once. This exercise creates a phased adoption plan with clear milestones.

```
Create a 90-day adoption plan for integrating AI into my Scrum Master practice.

STARTING POINT:
- I've completed the full AI for Scrum Masters course (Levels 1-6)
- I have access to Microsoft Copilot (M365), Copilot Studio, and Power Automate
- My team (Velocity Squad) is open to process improvements but skeptical of "AI hype"
- My manager (Sarah Chen, Delivery Lead) is supportive but wants to see measurable results

DESIGN A 3-PHASE PLAN:

**MONTH 1: AUTOMATE THE OBVIOUS (Weeks 1-4)**
- Goal: Eliminate top 3 time-sinks
- Focus: Sprint reports, meeting docs, blocker tracking
- Success metric: [define]
- Risk: [what could go wrong]
- Week-by-week breakdown

**MONTH 2: BUILD THE SYSTEM (Weeks 5-8)**
- Goal: Early warning system operational
- Focus: Signal detection, automated monitoring, proactive interventions
- Success metric: [define]
- Risk: [what could go wrong]
- Week-by-week breakdown

**MONTH 3: ELEVATE THE PRACTICE (Weeks 9-12)**
- Goal: Coaching practice and reflective system established
- Focus: Weekly coaching gym, reflective journaling, edge case library
- Success metric: [define]
- Risk: [what could go wrong]
- Week-by-week breakdown

For each phase, also include:
- **STAKEHOLDER COMMUNICATION** — What to tell the team and manager
- **MEASUREMENT** — How to track time savings and quality improvements
- **CHECKPOINT** — End-of-month review criteria (continue/adjust/abandon)
- **DEPENDENCY** — What needs to be true for this phase to work

End with a "DAY 91" vision: what does my practice look like when the plan is complete?
```

### What You Should See

A realistic plan that acknowledges:
- Month 1 is about quick wins that build confidence. Week 1: just the sprint report automation. Week 2: add standup notes. Week 3: add blocker tracking. Week 4: review and adjust.
- Month 2 requires more setup and may hit tool limitations. The plan should include fallback approaches.
- Month 3 is the hardest because coaching practice requires discipline, not just tools. The plan should include accountability mechanisms.
- Day 91 vision: "I spend 60% of my time on coaching, facilitation, and team development. Reporting and documentation happen in the background. I catch problems 2-3 days earlier than I used to. I have a weekly practice that makes me a better SM every month."

## Exercise 3: Skill Gap Assessment (5 min)

**Level: Intermediate**

You can't improve what you can't measure. This exercise assesses your current capabilities against an AI-Enabled SM Competency Model.

```
Help me assess my skills against an AI-Enabled Scrum Master Competency Model.

COMPETENCY DOMAINS:

1. **AI Fluency** — Prompt engineering, context design, output evaluation
2. **Automation Design** — Workflow chains, agent design, trigger-based systems
3. **Data-Driven Facilitation** — Using signals and metrics to guide ceremonies
4. **AI-Assisted Coaching** — Simulation practice, reflective journaling, feedback integration
5. **Ethical AI Practice** — Human judgment boundaries, privacy, transparency
6. **Scaling with AI** — Cross-team coordination, organizational patterns, knowledge sharing

For each domain, define:
- **NOVICE** — Can follow prompts from the course
- **PRACTITIONER** — Can adapt prompts to new situations
- **ADVANCED** — Can design new workflows and teach others
- **EXPERT** — Can evaluate AI tools critically and shape organizational AI strategy

SELF-ASSESSMENT (be honest):
- AI Fluency: I can write good prompts and get useful outputs. I sometimes struggle with complex multi-step chains.
- Automation Design: I've designed workflows in this course but haven't implemented them in production.
- Data-Driven Facilitation: I use velocity and burndown but don't systematically scan for team health signals.
- AI-Assisted Coaching: New to simulation practice. Haven't established a journaling habit yet.
- Ethical AI Practice: I understand the boundaries intellectually but haven't tested them in real situations.
- Scaling with AI: I work with one team. Haven't applied AI to cross-team coordination yet.

Based on my self-assessment:
1. Rate me in each domain (Novice/Practitioner/Advanced/Expert)
2. Identify my top 2 strengths and top 2 development priorities
3. For each development priority, recommend specific practice activities
4. Create a 6-month development goal with monthly milestones
5. Suggest how to get feedback on my progress (not just self-assessment)
```

### What You Should See

An honest assessment (likely Practitioner in AI Fluency, Novice-to-Practitioner in the rest) with:
- Development priorities that connect back to specific course modules for reinforcement
- Practice activities that are concrete and time-bound ("Run 2 coaching gym sessions per week for 4 weeks")
- Feedback sources: peer SM observation, team retrospective feedback, manager 1:1 input
- 6-month trajectory: from Practitioner to Advanced in AI Fluency and Automation, from Novice to Practitioner in Coaching and Scaling

> **Reference:** See `sample-files/practice-design/competency-model.md` for the full competency model with behavioral indicators at each level.

## Exercise 4: Staying Current (5 min)

**Level: Intermediate**

AI capabilities change fast. A prompt that doesn't work today might work in 3 months. A workflow you built might become unnecessary when a new feature launches. This exercise builds a system for staying current.

```
Help me build a system for evaluating new AI capabilities as they emerge.

CURRENT TOOLS:
- Microsoft Copilot (M365) — primary tool
- Copilot Studio — agent design (exploring)
- Power Automate — workflow automation
- Teams — communication hub

EVALUATION FRAMEWORK:
When a new AI feature or capability is announced, I need to assess:

1. **RELEVANCE** — Does this affect SM work specifically?
   - Score: High / Medium / Low / Not Relevant

2. **READINESS** — Is it available in my environment? Does it work reliably?
   - Score: Available Now / Preview / Announced / Rumored

3. **IMPACT** — How would this change my operating system?
   - Categories: Replaces existing workflow / Enhances existing workflow / Enables new capability / Nice to have

4. **EFFORT** — What does adoption require?
   - Score: Drop-in replacement / Requires configuration / Requires new skills / Requires organizational change

Design:
1. A one-page evaluation template I can fill out in 10 minutes
2. A monthly "What's New" review process (where to look, what to read, how long to spend)
3. Criteria for when to adopt immediately vs. wait vs. ignore
4. How to share findings with the SM Community of Practice
5. A "sunset" process for retiring workflows that new features make obsolete

Keep it lightweight. I don't want evaluating AI to become another time-sink.
```

### What You Should See

A practical evaluation system:
- Monthly time commitment: 30 minutes (15 min scanning sources, 15 min evaluating one feature)
- Sources: Microsoft 365 roadmap, Copilot changelog, SM community forums, one trusted AI newsletter
- Adoption criteria: Adopt immediately if (High Relevance + Available Now + Replaces existing workflow). Wait if (Preview or Requires new skills). Ignore if (Low Relevance or Requires organizational change you can't drive).
- Sharing format: 3-bullet summary posted in SM Community of Practice channel

> **Reference:** See `sample-files/practice-design/new-feature-evaluation-template.md` for the evaluation template.

## Exercise 5: Knowledge Base Contribution (5 min)

**Level: Advanced**

The best way to solidify your learning is to teach it. This exercise has you write up your most effective prompt chain for other Scrum Masters to reuse.

```
Help me write up my most effective prompt chain as a reusable contribution to the SM Community of Practice.

I want to document the Ceremony Automation Chain from Module 6.1 (the 4-step chain: agenda → notes template → action extraction → follow-up reminders).

FORMAT THE WRITE-UP AS:

**TITLE:** [Descriptive name]

**PROBLEM IT SOLVES:**
- What SM pain point does this address?
- How much time does it typically save?

**PREREQUISITES:**
- What tools are needed?
- What data/context must be available?

**THE CHAIN:**
- Step 1: [Prompt with placeholders for customization]
- Step 2: [Prompt building on Step 1 output]
- Step 3: [Prompt building on Step 2 output]
- Step 4: [Prompt building on Step 3 output]

**CUSTOMIZATION GUIDE:**
- What to change for different team sizes
- What to change for different ceremony types
- What to change for remote vs. co-located teams

**TIPS & GOTCHAS:**
- What works better than expected
- What doesn't work well (and workarounds)
- How to tell if the output needs heavy editing

**RESULTS:**
- My time savings (before vs. after)
- Quality comparison (stakeholder feedback)
- What I'd do differently next time

Write this as a practical, no-nonsense guide that a fellow SM could pick up and use in 15 minutes. No theory. No motivation. Just "here's what works."
```

### What You Should See

A clean, practical write-up that:
- Opens with the problem and time savings (not with "AI is transforming...")
- Provides copy-paste prompts with clear [PLACEHOLDER] markers
- Includes honest "gotchas" (e.g., "Step 3 works best if your raw notes include names — anonymous notes produce vague action items")
- Has a customization guide that acknowledges different contexts
- Could genuinely be shared in a Slack channel and used by another SM the same day

## Exercise 6: Course Capstone (2 min)

**Level: Advanced**

This is it. The final exercise of the entire course. No long prompt. No complex chain. Just reflection.

```
I've just completed a comprehensive course on using AI (Microsoft Copilot) as a Scrum Master. I started at Level 1 learning basic prompts and workspace setup. I progressed through ceremony facilitation, impediment management, team health, data-driven practices, scaling, and now AI-native SM work.

Help me complete these three sentences. Don't give me generic answers — make them specific to what an SM who completed this journey would actually feel and know.

Before this course, I used AI to ____________.

Now I use AI to ____________.

The biggest shift was ____________.

Then give me:
1. **ONE THING** to do tomorrow morning that puts this entire course into practice
2. **ONE QUESTION** to sit with over the next month as I develop my practice
3. **ONE COMMITMENT** to make to my team about how I'll use AI as their Scrum Master
```

### What You Should See

Honest, specific completions:

- "Before this course, I used AI to **draft emails and summarize meetings — basic productivity, nothing specific to my SM role.**"
- "Now I use AI to **run systematic signal detection on my team's health, prepare for coaching conversations I used to wing, automate the documentation that stole my attention from the humans in the room, and deliberately practice the hardest parts of my job in a safe environment.**"
- "The biggest shift was **realizing that AI doesn't make me a faster Scrum Master — it makes me a more present one. The time I save on reports is time I now spend actually looking at my team.**"

The one thing for tomorrow: "Run the signal scan prompt before standup. Just once. See what it surfaces that you would have missed."

The one question: "Am I spending the time I'm saving on the things that actually matter to my team?"

The one commitment: "I will be transparent with my team about how I use AI — what it helps me with, what I never delegate to it, and why the human parts of this role are non-negotiable."

> ### Signals to Watch For
>
> As you build your ongoing practice, watch for these signals:
>
> **Healthy practice evolution:**
> - Your prompts get shorter and more precise over time — you know exactly what you need
> - You start designing new workflows that aren't in any course
> - Other SMs ask you what you're doing differently
> - Your team health metrics improve and you can trace it to specific interventions you had time for because automation freed you up
>
> **Practice stagnation:**
> - You're using the same prompts from month 1 with no adaptation
> - You stopped the reflective journal after 2 weeks
> - You measure success by "time saved" instead of "impact on team"
> - You haven't shared anything with the SM community

## When This Doesn't Work

Building a sustainable practice is the hardest part:

- **The system feels like overhead.** Your operating system should save time on net. If it doesn't by week 3, simplify. Cut it to the 2 highest-value activities and rebuild from there.
- **You fall off the rhythm.** Everyone does. The failure mode isn't missing a day — it's treating that as proof the system doesn't work. Miss Monday? Do a compressed version Tuesday. Don't restart from zero.
- **Your team context changes.** New team, new project, new organization. Your operating system needs a hard reset. Run Exercise 1 again with the new context. The skills transfer; the prompts don't.
- **AI capabilities shift.** A new Copilot feature makes one of your workflows obsolete. Good. Use the Staying Current evaluation (Exercise 4) to assess and adapt. A system that never changes is a system that's falling behind.
- **You can't measure the impact.** Some benefits are hard to quantify ("I caught a morale issue 3 days earlier"). Keep a simple log: one sentence per week on "what AI helped me do that I wouldn't have done otherwise." The pattern will emerge.
